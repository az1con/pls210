The crevasse The puzzle

Taught to care, but unsure we can act

We are taught to care

“Don't litter.” “Protect Mother Nature.”

Teens in Kazakhstan name caring for the environment as the top thing young people should do for their country.

But unsure we can act

In the same study, motivation to act is the weakest part. Many say “I don't know.”

Public hearings exist, but activists say they were flawed. Speaking up gets called “hype”.

Gerfanova, Rudik & Nemchenko 2025 · KazTAG 2026

The peak we aim for Research question

How do young people in Kazakhstan understand and narrate their own capacity to influence environmental policy?

Understandhow they make sense of their own power

The compass Epistemological positioning

Interpretivism

Understanding what something means to the people living it.

  • I ask how young people see their power, and only they can tell me.
  • A 1–5 scale of “how much influence can someone like you have?” decides the meaning for them.
  • So: open conversations, building understanding from their stories.

Schwartz-Shea & Yanow 2012 · Boulianne & Ohme 2022

A new route Contribution

What this study adds

  • A new place: Central Asia, barely on the research map.
  • New voices: young people who act and those who stay quiet.
  • The policy question, in their own words.

Why it matters: to see why caring about nature does, or doesn't, become a sense of having a say.

Camp 1 Literature review: where scholars agree

Young people act outside formal politics, and power answers with dismissal

Sloam, Pickard & Henn 2022 · Boulianne & Ohme 2022 · Hockey 2025 · Collins 2021 · Hwang 2026

  • Care flows into “Do-It-Ourselves” politics: online groups, boycotts, everyday choices.
  • Authorities call it a “game”; Korean activists feel “youth-washed”.

How solid? Firm, but built in established democracies. It shows what youth do, not what it means to them.

Camp 2 Literature review: debate one

Does being dismissed fuel young people, or drain them?

Fuel

Anger at disrespectful politicians cancels the drop in willingness to act; grief turns into outrage.

Šerek et al. 2026 · Hockey 2025

Drain

Youth feel unheard and need “safe spaces” to keep going; motivation stays low.

Hwang 2026 · Gerfanova et al. 2025

My judgment: partly a split of method: imagined insults in experiments vs. real lives in interviews. Nobody asks how youth read being dismissed.

Camp 3 Literature review: debate two

What counts as real influence?

Real

Youth action is changing politics; shopping and banking choices are levers.

Sloam et al. 2022 · Boulianne & Ohme 2022 · Hockey 2025

Symbolic

Some activism may be a “cool” trend; policy invites youth but offers few real seats.

Collins 2021 · Hwang 2026

My judgment: they talk past each other. “Influence” means hope, a survey score, or a seat at the table, and the researcher decides, not the young person.

The unclimbed peak The gap

In these studies, young people's capacity to influence is…

  • counted
  • predicted
  • judged by researchers
  • assumed

Nobody has asked young people in Kazakhstan, active and not, what it means to be able, or unable, to shape environmental policy.

a score to measure → a story to understand

Where I stand on the slope Positionality

Partly an insider

I have started and joined environmental activities in Kazakhstan since I was 13.

  • Helps: shared language and trust with activists.
  • Risks: hearing my own frustration; polite answers to “the activist”.
  • So: ask their meaning first, include those who don't act, note my own reactions.
